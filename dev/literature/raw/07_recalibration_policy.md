# Review item 7: calibration drift, recalibration policy, sepsis deployments, quantile versus probability thresholds (search report, 31 Oct 2026)

Access caveat recorded by the searcher: 29 abstract-level searches; full texts (PMC, PubMed, arXiv, FDA, JMIR, medRxiv, BMC, ScienceDirect) not reachable; [unverified] items rest on secondary summaries or recall.

## Drift over time

- Davis 2017 (JAMIA 24:1052): seven hospital-acquired AKI models trained on 2003 VA admissions and tested over nine years; discrimination held, calibration decayed with growing over-prediction driven by a falling event rate (calibration-in-the-large drift); regression models drifted more than ML; recommends updating triggered by observed drift rather than a fixed schedule.
- Hickey 2013 (EuroSCORE, 317,292 operations): observed-to-expected ratio fell from 0.76 to 0.37 over ten years with discrimination preserved [citation unverified]; periodic refitting or dynamic updating judged necessary.
- Nestor 2019 (MLHC): train-past test-future AUROC drops up to 0.29 with raw features, 0.06 with clinical-concept aggregation, driven by the 2008 charting-system switch in MIMIC.
- arXiv 2203.16452: an RNN sepsis model on MIMIC-IV decayed from AUC 0.729 to 0.525 across a decade [unverified].
- Rahmani 2023 (Int J Med Inform 173): retrained models beat static ones in every simulated drift scenario; retraining every couple of months or several thousand patients judged adequate; concept shift needs a full rebuild; COVID produced the largest shift.
- JMIR 2024 (26:e51409): at a fixed threshold, alert rates rose over years at one hospital despite stable AUROC and incidence, not at a second; sepsis alert rates more than doubled during COVID (Wong 2021) [magnitude unverified].

## Drift across sites

Moor 2023 (within-site 0.846, cross-site 0.761, 0.807 with 10 % local fine-tuning); Rockenschaub 2024 (AUROC loss up to 0.200 at unseen hospitals; multicentre training closed most of the gap; domain-generalisation methods did not help; calibration not in the abstract); Wardi 2021 (transfer learning with a small local sample restored AUROC above 0.8 at a second site) [fraction unverified].

## Updating methods and sample size

Steyerberg 2004 (parsimonious updating, intercept then slope; full revision only with large samples and shrinkage); Vergouwe 2017 (closed-testing ladder original, intercept, intercept plus slope, revision; the closed test chose intercept-only); Davis 2019 and 2020 (nonparametric extension; drift detection with adaptive windows); Schnellinger 2021 (recalibration gave the most consistent gains across update intervals; refitting more variable); Tanner 2023 (intercept-only most effective at small n); Riley 2021 (precise external validation at a 1.8 % event rate needed 9,835 participants and 177 events; the calibration slope is the binding constraint); Niculescu-Mizil and Caruana 2005 (isotonic overfits when data are scarce; Platt scaling safer); Guo 2017 (temperature scaling corrects slope only, not intercept, and intercept shift is the dominant drift); Jenkins 2021 and Feng 2022 (updating is ad hoc; continual monitoring with statistical process control; methods still nascent).

## Sepsis deployments

Epic Sepsis Model: Wong 2021 (Michigan; AUROC 0.63 versus vendor 0.76 to 0.83; a fixed score alerted 18 % of all hospitalised patients, sensitivity 33 %, PPV 12 %); Lyons 2023 (about 807,000 encounters, nine hospitals, incidence 4.8 to 12.0 %; discrimination fell with incidence and comorbidity burden) [range unverified]; Kamran 2024 (AUROC 0.62 overall, 0.47 restricted to pre-treatment predictions); Epic's 2022 version is locally trained per site [unverified details]. TREWS (Adams 2022, Nat Med; 590,736 patients, five hospitals; confirmation within 3 h associated with a 3.3-point absolute mortality reduction; threshold and recalibration policy not retrieved). COMPOSER (conformal abstention; deployment mortality −1.9 points). Kaiser AAM (Escobar 2020, NEJM; threshold chosen to yield about one new alert per day per 35 patients, workload-anchored; C 0.82, sensitivity 49 %; 16 % lower mortality; no recalibration schedule found). Duke Sepsis Watch (Bedoya 2020, Sendak 2020; trained 2014 to 2015, temporally validated 2018, C 0.88, advantage retained about 2.5 years later). Regulation: FDA predetermined change control plan guidance (December 2024) allows retraining as a prespecified modification with acceptance criteria; FDA lifecycle draft (January 2025) [status unverified]; ONC HTI-1 (December 2023) source attributes including local validity and maintenance; Joint Commission and CHAI guidance (September 2025) on local validation and risk-based monitoring [secondary summaries].

## Fixed alarm fraction versus fixed probability threshold

No head-to-head study found. Indirect evidence: the dominant drift mode is intercept shift with preserved discrimination (Davis 2017; EuroSCORE; JMIR 2024 alert-rate creep at stable AUROC; Wong's 18 % alert rate at a vendor-fixed score; incidence 4.8 to 12 % across Lyons' nine hospitals). A quantile operating point is invariant to monotone miscalibration, holds workload constant by construction and lets PPV track local prevalence; its cost is that a true prevalence change silently moves sensitivity. Kaiser and a palliative-care decision analysis both anchored the threshold on workload. Conclusion: workload stability favours the quantile rule, as a theoretical argument with consistent indirect evidence, not a demonstrated result.

## Recommended policy statement (for the discussion; the event cut-points are a synthesis, not a single-source figure)

Monitor the observed-to-expected ratio, calibration slope and realised alarm fraction quarterly with statistical process control, updating when drift is observed rather than on a calendar; apply the closed-testing ladder: intercept-only correction once about 50 to 100 local onset events have accrued, intercept plus slope at about 150 to 200 events, isotonic or model revision only with at least 1,000 calibration cases and shrinkage; carry the quantile operating point across sites so alarm burden is fixed by construction and report local PPV and sensitivity at that fraction; treat any retraining as a prespecified modification under a change-control protocol. A cadence of a few months or several thousand patients is consistent with Rahmani 2023 and Schnellinger 2021.

## Stage-2 analyses warranted, cost, recommendation

(a) Threshold-transport comparison on eICU: carry both the MIMIC-derived quantile and the corresponding fixed probability to each hospital; report per-hospital alarm fraction, sensitivity and PPV dispersion (about one day; fills the gap above and supports the protocol's central design choice). (b) Temporal validation in MIMIC-IV by anchor-year group (2008 to 2010, 2011 to 2013, 2014 to 2016, 2017 to 2019, 2020 to 2022 in v3): train early, test later; AUROC, observed-to-expected ratio, slope and alarm fraction under both threshold rules (one to two days). (c) Recalibration sample-size curve on eICU: 25 to 400 local events per large hospital; none versus intercept versus logistic versus isotonic (three to four days; optional or reduced). Recommendation: add (a) and (b); (c) optional; everything else discussion only; nothing warrants dropping the quantile design.
